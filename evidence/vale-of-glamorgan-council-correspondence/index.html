Sirs/Ma’am, I’m writing in relation to land formerly known as *Great House Farm, Llandough (Vale of Glamorgan)*, subsequently developed for residential use and historically connected with the proceedings culminating in *BP v Buckler (Court of Appeal, 1987)*. My purpose is to place on record a *chronological summary of unresolved land-governance issues* which may be material to the integrity of the planning history relied upon for that site. In brief and as reflected in public records: – In *July 1974*, proceedings involving my grandmother and BP both asserting ownership, were adjourned and did not result in a judicial final determination. – On *31 October 1974*, a further listed hearing did not proceed substantively, following correspondence sent that morning by BP entities. No judgment on title was issued. My nan requested the audience with the judge the day before, with her lawyer. But that got leaked, the press descended on our farm (not that I can find evidence of news articles from said press), and the following morning the two letters from BP arrived to our solicitor, effectively squashing the court hearing and audience with the judge my nan had sought, to make her case of ownership. Her reply to both BP letters (one from BP Pension Trust Ltd and one from BP Properties Ltd), that morning of the 31st of October, was also ‘I own the land’. – Throughout this period, my grandmother was *excluded from dealings* concerning land we asserted we owned, while the opposing party continued to *assert ownership*, notwithstanding the absence of a concluded judicial finding. – In or around *1982*, the land was registered in BP’s name, despite the earlier proceedings never having resolved the competing claims nor having resulted in findings adverse to my grandmother. – My grandmother then died in *1983*, after the registration had taken place, without compensation and without any final adjudication of her claim. – In the *1987 appeal*, the Court referred to correspondence as having been replied to (albeit not to the correct corporate entity – which is ultimately why we lost BP vs Buckler 1987), yet in my fathers appeal that followed, whereby he argued that surely BP Pension Trust should have been the claimant not BP Properties, the same judge from the case dismissed the appeal citing that both companies were effectively the same. This is all evidence on the face of the public record of these proceedings. – By that stage, and in the absence of a documentary title which had effectively been overtaken by registration, my family was driven to advance arguments in adverse possession as a *fallback position*, which itself should have assured our continued occupancy. The cumulative effect was that my family was *displaced*, the land was developed, and no compensation was ever paid, despite ownership never having been conclusively decided in court. Whether intentionally or not, the process operated in a manner where asserted ownership became treated as settled fact. I am not alleging misconduct, nor am I seeking retrospective remedies from the Authority. I am simply asking whether, given the above sequence, it would be appropriate for the Authority to: – note that historic planning decisions were taken on the basis of *assumed title*, and – record that the underlying ownership dispute was *never finally adjudicated*, for the sake of transparency and future governance integrity. This correspondence is intended to be factual, measured, and non-adversarial. I am happy to supply references to the relevant hearings, registrations, or judgments if that assists your records. But what I’m highlighting here is all on public record and in the public eye. Yours faithfully, Sion Buckler
